# Vision

People sit on ideas because sharing feels risky. Spark inverts that: the timestamped public page *is* your receipt — the same logic as defensive publication in patent law. The earlier and more openly you share, the stronger your claim.

## The orchestration layer is homeless

Even the most technical projects keep their artifacts in GitHub, but the *orchestration* — why this architecture, what to try next, how pieces compose — happens on whiteboards and scattered docs with no public, versioned home. That layer is what survives commoditization: when anyone can generate a codebase, the differentiating record is the sequence of decisions that shaped it.

And every idea has this layer, not just software: a restaurant concept, a research program, a documentary. Code was one execution medium among many. Spark is the home for the layer above all execution media.

## Ideas as executable artifacts

A Spark page is not a brochure; it is a context bundle an agent can execute. Fork an idea, clone its bundle into Claude Code or similar, and instantiate the project. GitHub stores the output layer (code); Spark stores the intent layer. This also makes merging real: contributions are concrete diffs on spec files, not vague suggestions.

## Credit, not exclusivity

Attribution on Spark records who said and did what, when — not ownership. Multiple people can pursue the same idea; what differentiates them is team, activity, and progress, which is exactly what Spark surfaces. A page with no execution behind it is just a timestamp, and activity-weighting leaves it invisible.
